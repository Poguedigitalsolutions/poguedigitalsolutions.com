# What Adam Sandler's Click Teaches Us About Letting AI Run Your Life

**The short answer:** In the 2006 comedy *Click*, Adam Sandler plays a man who uses a magic remote to skip the boring parts of his life. Then the remote learns his habits and starts skipping for him. Artificial intelligence (AI) tools that learn your preferences can do the same thing. They save time, and they also copy your shortcuts. The fix is to keep your hand on the remote for the choices that matter most.

## What Click is about

Most of us have owned a TV remote with a fast-forward button. *Click* asks a simple question: what if that button worked on your life?

The movie came out on June 23, 2006, from Columbia Pictures, directed by Frank Coraci. Sandler plays Michael Newman, an architect who works too much and keeps missing time with his wife and kids. One night he goes looking for a universal remote and meets Morty, a strange store clerk played by Christopher Walken. Morty hands him a remote that can pause, rewind, and fast-forward his own life.

At first it feels like a gift. Michael skips arguments, traffic, and dinners he finds dull. He gets more done at work. On paper, his life gets more efficient.

## The turn: the remote learns his habits

Here is the part people tend to forget. Michael keeps skipping the same kinds of moments, so the remote starts skipping them on its own. Morty explains that the remote programs itself based on what Michael chose before.

Michael never told it to take over. He just used it the same way, over and over, until his habits became its settings. He ends up on autopilot, losing years he never got to live.

The remote never did anything Michael hadn't taught it. That is what makes the story useful today.

## Where AI works like Michael's remote

Many of the tools we use every day learn from what we do. The more we use them, the more they decide for us.

- **Streaming apps** start the next episode before we choose to watch it.
- **Email and text apps** suggest replies, and we tap the first one.
- **AI assistants** draft a letter, a post, or a plan, and we send it without changing a word.
- **Calendar and shopping tools** learn our patterns and fill in the next step for us.

Each one saves a few seconds or minutes. None of them is a problem by itself. The risk shows up when we stop noticing the choices being made. Like Michael, we can end up on autopilot without ever deciding to give up control.

## The name for what happened to Michael: automation bias

Researchers have a name for this pattern. **Automation bias** is the habit of trusting an automated system too much and checking its work too little.

The idea came out of aviation research in the 1990s. Kathleen Mosier and Linda Skitka studied how airline pilots used automated cockpit aids. They found pilots sometimes followed the machine even when it was wrong, or missed a problem because the machine didn't flag it. A 2012 review in the *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association* found the same pattern in health care, where doctors and nurses use computer systems to help make decisions.

Here is the teachable moment. Automation bias is a human habit. The tool doesn't have to be broken for it to cause harm. Michael's remote worked perfectly. It did exactly what he had trained it to do. The trouble was that he stopped watching.

## How to keep your hand on the remote

You don't have to throw the remote away. AI is a great tool when you stay in charge of it. A few habits help:

1. **Decide what you never skip.** Family dinners, a hard talk with a friend, the first draft of something personal. Make your own list and protect it.
2. **Read before you send.** Treat an AI draft as a starting point. Change it until it sounds like you.
3. **Fact-check every time.** If AI gives you a number, a date, or a name, look it up at the source before you repeat it.
4. **Turn off one autopilot setting.** Pick one, like autoplay on your streaming app, and see what you choose when the choice is yours again.
5. **Ask yourself who taught the tool.** If its suggestions feel off, look at the habits you've been feeding it.

## The moments worth living through

*Click* is a comedy, but it lands because most of us know the feeling. We all have days we wish we could skip. Some of those days turn out to hold the things we miss most later: a kid's question, a long talk, a slow dinner with people we love.

AI can give us back hours we used to waste. I want those hours spent on the people and work that matter. So use the remote. Just keep it in your own hand.
