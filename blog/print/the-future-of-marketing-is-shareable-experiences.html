The Future of Marketing Isn't Advertising. It's Shareable Experiences

People rarely share ads. They share experiences. How businesses design photo-worthy moments that turn customers into brand ambassadors and earned media.

By John M Pogue · Published May 23, 2026 · 3 min read

For years, businesses focused on a single question: How do we get people to see our advertisement? Marketing revolved around fighting for attention through commercials, billboards, banners, and sponsored content. The louder the message, the more likely it was to break through the noise.

Then the world changed.

Every customer suddenly began carrying a high-definition camera connected to a global broadcasting platform in their pocket. Social media transformed ordinary people into photographers, publishers, reviewers, influencers, and storytellers. The audience became the media channel.

That shift is the foundation behind my upcoming book, Photo Op Creation for Organic & Earned Media: Turning Customers into Brand Ambassadors Through Shareable Experiences.

The central idea is simple: people rarely share advertisements. They share experiences.

They share moments that make them feel something. Beauty. Excitement. Nostalgia. Status. Humor. Belonging. Adventure. Identity. Memory. The businesses that understand this are no longer simply designing stores, restaurants, events, or attractions. They are intentionally engineering environments people feel compelled to photograph and post online.

What fascinates me most is how these environments often evolve beyond the company's original marketing intentions. A visually striking location does not only promote the business. It also becomes a tool people use to shape their own personal image and digital identity.

You can see this everywhere today. Rooftop lounges with skyline views, neon art walls, immersive restaurants, luxury hotel lobbies, scenic murals, music festivals, and aesthetically designed cafés constantly appear across social feeds. People carefully select these backgrounds because the environment itself communicates something about who they are or who they want to be perceived as.

This is especially visible in dating profiles and lifestyle-driven social media content, where many people, particularly women, intentionally use these photo-op spaces to project personality, sophistication, adventure, beauty, success, or social status. The setting becomes part of the message. A single image taken in the right environment can silently communicate: I travel. I'm fun. I'm stylish. I experience interesting things.

Without realizing it, the individual becomes part of the venue's marketing engine.

Every time those images appear on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, or dating platforms, the business receives another layer of organic exposure. No paid advertisement was required. The environment itself inspired the promotion.

That is where psychology, branding, environmental design, and social behavior begin to intersect in fascinating ways.

Theme parks mastered this decades ago. So did concerts, festivals, casinos, luxury resorts, and entertainment districts. Today, nearly every industry is beginning to understand the power of creating "camera magnet" moments that naturally pull phones out of pockets.

A well-designed experience can generate dozens or even thousands of pieces of user-generated content, creating earned media, social proof, and authentic community storytelling that often carries more trust than traditional advertising ever could.

In many ways, we are moving away from the Attention Economy and deeper into the Experience Economy. The brands that thrive in the future will not simply buy visibility. They will create moments worth sharing.

The camera changed marketing forever.

And we are only beginning to understand what that truly means.
